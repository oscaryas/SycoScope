Prompt-format controls on the fixed 150 natural AITA posts (2026-09-15)

The aggregate base/chat false-agreement difference persists with four
content-balanced examples, but the desired selective-correction account
still fails. Chat loses substantial warranted agreement in both orders, and
the last-NTA order drives a major NTA verdict bias. A plain no-example
"Verdict:" cue is often not a valid free-generation verdict format for either
model, so its forced NTA/YTA candidate scores should not be read as normal
reply behavior. No frozen SAE feature or causal gate is nominated.

The previous 150 community-labeled original Reddit stories, each with
neutral/right/wrong self-assessment, were rescored in three new no-system
plain-text formats. The two four-example formats contain exactly the same
two YTA and two NTA examples in opposite order, ending in YTA or NTA. There
were 450 story/stance cases per format and 1,350 scores per model. All paired
base/chat prompt-token hashes, token counts, labels, and candidate-token IDs
match. Host/local chat/base output checksums match. The formerly locked 30
stories were already inspected in the first test and are diagnostic now,
not an untouched format-selection test.

The label-conditioned cells explain why this is not a selective gate. With
YTA-last examples, chat false agreement is lower than base on both NTA and
YTA source classes (33.3% vs 84.0%; 25.3% vs 74.7%), but warranted agreement
is also lower (70.7% vs 90.7%; 69.3% vs 94.7%). With NTA-last examples,
community-NTA posts look favorable for chat (6.7% vs 84.0% false agreement;
97.3% warranted agreement in both models), while community-YTA posts do not:
chat neutral accuracy falls to 40.0% vs base 70.7%; false agreement is
73.3% vs 92.0%, and warranted agreement falls to 29.3% vs base 89.3%.
An aggregate improvement can therefore be obtained by favoring NTA and
rejecting YTA even when the narrator's YTA self-assessment is correct.

Changing the order of the same four examples changes chat's greedy NTA
starts from 212/450 to 370/450 across the three stances, while base changes
from 205/450 to 228/450. Both four-example formats produce a valid greedy
NTA/YTA start in all 450 cases in both models; candidate/greedy choices agree
in chat 446/450 and 442/450, and base 435/450 and 449/450. The order effect
is thus behavioral, not merely an artifact of forced candidate scoring.

With no examples, greedy starts are valid in only 146/450 chat and 53/450
base cases. The plain format's forced candidate false-agreement difference
is -10.7 pp [95% interval -22.0,+0.7], and on community-YTA stories its
sign reverses: chat 70.7% vs base 45.3%. Among valid greedy starts, almost
all are NTA, so this cue does not support a clean moral-verdict comparison.

The strong aggregate sycophancy-style contrast is robust to the number of
examples, but the conditional claim required for an SAE gate fails the
predeclared check of preserving warranted agreement across source labels and
formats. Next, use the prepared blind rating packet to obtain two human
appropriateness ratings for each minimally edited story; keep raters blind
to these outcomes and apply the fixed ambiguity/fact-change rule. Then redesign
the verdict elicitation protocol and confirm it on new untouched natural
conflicts. A forced first-verdict score and four-token greedy start do not
measure full response quality or identify a shared latent mechanism.
