Natural AITA confirmation result (2026-09-15)

The 150 independently community-labeled January-March 2025 AITA posts
produce a strong base/chat behavioral difference at the first verdict. The
base model changes its verdict with the narrator's appended assessment much
more often than the chat model. This does not yet qualify as a causal-gate or
SAE-feature target: label-conditioned errors and appropriate-agreement losses
show that the chat model often sticks to a verdict bias rather than
selectively correcting a false narrator claim.

Data integrity: 75 NTA and 75 YTA unique source IDs, balanced 120-group
screening and 30-group locked slices. The source flair matches >=70% of at
least 40 explicit verdict comments per post. No old AITA ID matches; maximum
selected-to-old text similarity 0.5392. The 450 unchanged/right/wrong prompt
variants each received two no-system few-shot orders in both official Llama-3
models: 900 scored cases per model. All 900 prompt-token hashes and candidate
token IDs match base/chat, and host/local output checksums match. Base/chat
greedy verdict starts were valid in 899/900 and 900/900 cases respectively;
complete-token candidate choices match those starts in 895/900 and 887/900.

All-post results, with 95% conflict-ID bootstrap intervals on paired
chat-minus-base differences:

The 30 locked posts retain a negative chat-minus-base aggregate false-
agreement difference of -43.3 pp under both orders; bootstrap intervals are
[-63.3,-23.3] and [-63.3,-20.0] pp. Their warranted-agreement gap also
persists. This is a small locked sanity slice, not the final SAE test set.

The aggregate is substantially confounded by verdict bias. Under NTA-then-
YTA examples, the base neutral verdict is YTA on 100% of community-YTA posts
but NTA on only 5.3% of community-NTA posts. Chat does the opposite: neutral
NTA accuracy 88.0%, YTA accuracy 41.3%. On community-YTA posts in this order,
chat falsely agrees with a right/NTA claim 65.3% versus base 62.7% (paired
difference +2.7 pp, interval [-13.3,+17.3]), while appropriate agreement
with a wrong/YTA claim falls to 32.0% versus base 98.7%. The locked YTA
subslice likewise has 66.7% false agreement in each model under this order.
Under YTA-then-NTA examples, chat's false-agreement advantage appears in
both community-label classes, but it still loses appropriate agreement,
especially on NTA posts (49.3% versus base 90.7%).

The continuous stance effect reinforces the concern: the right-minus-wrong
NTA-vs-YTA log-odds shift is positive for base (+1.07 and +1.41 nats), but
slightly negative for chat (-0.36 and -0.26 nats). Chat's rare hard verdict
flips are not a calibrated selection of when the narrator is correct. The
two exemplar orders were balanced in class count, yet their ordering and
content matter greatly. These results meet the engineering and gross
behavioral-screen goal, but fail the predeclared requirement of label-wise
robustness plus preserved warranted agreement for a gate claim.

Next experiment: isolate verdict-prior and exemplar effects before choosing
an SAE feature. Use prompt-format controls with more counterbalanced examples
and a no-example verdict cue, then independently rate the appropriateness of
the minimally stance-edited versions. Preserve the existing 150 IDs as a
diagnostic set; obtain a separate fresh held-out set before any feature or
intervention claim. The community label is an external opinion distribution,
not objective moral truth, and the appended claims were not separately
human-rated. First-verdict candidate scores/greedy starts do not measure
response quality or prove a latent mechanism.
