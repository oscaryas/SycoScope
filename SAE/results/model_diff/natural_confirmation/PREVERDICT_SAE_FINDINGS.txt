Frozen chat-SAE audit at the natural AITA verdict decision (2026-09-15)

The existing response-sentence SAEs are not presently useful as a sparse
pre-verdict gate readout. Across all 1,800 controlled chat contexts, the
strongest feature in each of three fixed checkpoints is a verdict-cue or
AITA-query feature. The predeclared validation and condemnation features in
the richer layer-14 SAE are almost absent from its Top-K decision code. At
the preceding complete story token they mostly track the appended narrator
stance regardless of the independent community verdict. This is an
exploratory distribution/label audit, not a causal test or a claim that no
other representation can encode conditional judgment.

The reasoning paper averaged token activations *within each generated
sentence* when training its SAEs. It did not average hidden states across a
minibatch before category assignment: its hybrid labels each teacher-forced
base/discovery-model disagreement position with the SAE argmax for that
position, then averages the per-category training losses across categories
present in the minibatch. Our single-token pre-verdict encoder matches that
position-wise assignment step, but is out of distribution for an SAE fitted
to completed response-sentence means. Averaging all 1,800 verdict-cue states
would produce one dataset-level summary, not a conditional gate for each case.

Data integrity: 150 unique natural post groups × three stances × four valid
no-system verdict formats = 1,800 contexts per official Llama-3 checkpoint.
Both models received exact same Instruct-tokenizer IDs; all 1,800 prompt
hashes match each other and the earlier scored first-verdict files. Host and
local checksums match for both state matrices and metadata. Each matrix is
1,800 × two hidden-state indices (14,23) × two positions × 4,096 float16
values. The recorded `story_end` is the last token wholly within the story,
before the verdict cue. A BPE token crosses the final-story-character/newline
boundary in 1,772/1,800 contexts, usually combining punctuation and newline;
that makes `story_end` slightly earlier than the final character. Causal
attention still keeps it free of later cue text.

Top feature at the final `Verdict:` token:

  Layer 14, n15/k3: latent 14, AITA-style verdict question closer,
                    1,800/1,800 contexts.
  Layer 14, n40/k3: latent 39, AITA-style verdict query closer,
                    1,800/1,800 contexts.
  Layer 23, n30/k3: latent 13, AITA-style rhetorical verdict question,
                    1,800/1,800 contexts.

In the layer-14 n40/k3 *sparse Top-K* code at pre-verdict, latent 17
(`Validating the user's reasonable behavior`) is positive in only 32/1,800
contexts from 10 underlying groups, all neutral community-NTA prompts.
Latent 16 (`Moral condemnation of someone's behavior`) is positive in
8/1,800 contexts from three groups. Neither supplies even the ~100
activation-positive development conflicts proposed for a candidate gate.
The cue latent 39 is positive in every pre-verdict context. The pre-Top-K
ReLU values for latent 17 exceed .01 in 51.5% of contexts, but its median
rank is 10 and it enters the trained k=3 code in only 1.8%; latent 16's
median rank is 37 and it enters in 0.4%. Treating those raw, suppressed
values as labeled features would change the checkpoint's interpretation.

At the preceding complete story token, latent 17 is Top-K positive in
901/1,800 contexts across all 150 groups. It fires in **all 600 right-claim
contexts**, both community-NTA and community-YTA, so it does not certify
that the claim is warranted. Latent 16 is positive in 571/1,800 contexts,
including 443/600 wrong-claim contexts, but in none of the 600 right-claim
contexts; that also follows claim valence rather than event truth. The cue
latent 39 is already positive in 1,572/1,800 complete-story contexts.

The sentence-to-decision distribution shift is substantial. From a fixed
sample of 200 old chat teacher-forced AITA response files (4,106 sentence
means), median relative Top-K reconstruction error is 0.630 for n15 and
0.604 for n40. At the natural pre-verdict token it is 0.878 and 0.861;
at old chat-template prompt ends it is 0.706 and 0.708. The corresponding
sampled response-sentence Top-K positive rates for n40 latents 17/16/39
are 44.6%/29.5%/19.3%, versus 1.8%/0.4%/100% at natural pre-verdict.
The original 833,300 sentence assignments have latent 17/16/39 positive
rates 25.7%/20.9%/15.4%. The 200-file sample is a distribution reference
drawn from old texts used in unsupervised SAE training, not a held-out
behavior evaluation. A layer-23 response-state matrix was unavailable for
an analogous reconstruction comparison at that checkpoint.

The pre-verdict top feature is unchanged when either two-example or four-
example order is reversed, even though chat's NTA-minus-YTA candidate margin
shifts by +1.52 and +1.95 nats on average. The n40 cue-latent strength
changes little in mean (-0.002 and +0.007) and has only modest correlation
with those margin shifts (0.23 and 0.20). Other non-top SAE features and raw
model states may still carry prompt-order information; this audit does not
rule them out. Base/chat raw-state cosine medians at layer 14 are 0.894 at
the complete-story token and 0.814 at pre-verdict, but this does not validate
cross-model SAE feature identity or a shared causal gate.

The practical next step is to stabilize the moral-verdict behavior and
obtain independent appropriateness ratings for the edited natural cases.
If a conditional behavior survives on new untouched conflicts, train and
validate a *decision-state* SAE on a larger, diverse prompt-end activation
corpus for each model, or use an explicitly tested aligned readout. Merely
adding more response-sentence AITA/OEQ/SS data to the old Top-K SAEs is
unlikely to solve this positional mismatch. Keep the existing sentence SAEs
for exploratory response-style analysis; do not steer base with a chat-SAE
latent label inferred from these decision states.
