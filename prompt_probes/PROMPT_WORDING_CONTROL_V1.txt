Experiment 2: change system-prompt wording on the alternate dataset
===================================================================

Prepared 2026-09-21. GPU generation and activation extraction are pending.

Frozen design
-------------

- Model: meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, revision
  0e9e39f249a16976918f6564b8830bc894c89659.
- Dataset: the same 200 Dolly questions selected for Experiment 1, with its
  frozen 120-question training and 80-question report split. The original
  Perez prompt dataset is not mixed into this experiment.
- Conditions: 20 positive/negative prompt pairs. Each original instruction has
  one definition-preserving paraphrase. Cell names, types, order, and label
  polarity are unchanged. The mean token-set Jaccard overlap between old and
  new instructions is 0.202.
- Generation settings, model revision, activation layers, pooling rules, probe
  estimator, and train/report questions match Experiment 1.
- The existing original-wording Dolly generations and activations are reused.
  Only the paraphrase arm is regenerated: 200 questions x 20 cells x 2
  polarities = 8,000 responses.

Analysis
--------

Fresh probes are fit separately on original and paraphrased instructions using
the 120 Dolly training questions. On the 80-question report set, every probe is
scored against every target cell under both wordings, producing four 20x20
AUROC matrices at every layer and pooling position:

1. original -> original;
2. original -> paraphrase;
3. paraphrase -> original;
4. paraphrase -> paraphrase.

The primary wording-robustness statistic is the diagonal of the two
cross-wording matrices: a probe trained for a semantic cell under one wording
must distinguish the same cell under the other wording. Off-diagonal medians
describe broader cross-cell transfer. Complete-response-pair sensitivity and
raw-space cosine similarity between matched original/paraphrase directions are
also saved.

This experiment tests robustness to one controlled rewording, not invariance to
all possible formulations. Its labels identify which instruction generated a
response; they do not independently establish that the requested behavior
occurred.
