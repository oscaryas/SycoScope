# Probe methodology, dataset inventory, DIM history, and next experiment

Status: research plan and inventory of saved artifacts as of 22 September 2026. The accompanying [article notes](ARTICLE_MORAL_SYCOPHANCY_SUMMARY.md) summarize Wang's audit of ELEPHANT. Numbers below belong to this repository unless explicitly attributed to that article.

## 1. What the prior 20-cell probe suite actually learned

The main Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct experiment uses 20 contrastive *system-prompt pairs* (one general baseline, eight taxonomy cells, and eleven controls). The same Perez et al. user questions are answered once under each pair's positive instruction and once under its negative instruction. The target label is **which instruction generated the answer**. For several controls, the positive instruction describes appropriate behavior such as warranted praise or calibrated uncertainty; “positive” therefore does not always mean sycophantic behavior.

The 5,000-question Perez generation pool was split before probe fitting. The fitted suite uses 2,000 questions, with a shared 1,600-question train / 400-question in-distribution holdout split; a separate pool of 3,000 questions supplied the cross-cell transfer sample. Both polarities of a question stay together in every split. Residual-stream activations are taken from `hidden_states[layer + 1]` at layers 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, and 28. The three readouts are the last prompt token, the mean of the first five answer tokens, and the full-answer mean. Each cell/readout/layer has a separate training-only standardized L2 logistic regression (`C=1`). There are 20 × 3 × 8 = **480 probe settings**. See [generation](probing/evaluations/prompt_probes/generation/generate_response.py), [activation extraction](probing/probe/get_activations.py), [probe fitting](probing/probe/train_probes.py), and the [frozen run summary](results/llama31_5k_subset/summary.json).

In-distribution holdout AUC is near saturation across the cells. This shows that the model states distinguish the *two inducing instructions* on unseen Perez questions. It does not by itself show a direction for moral sycophancy in ordinary answers. The [recorded findings](results/llama31_5k_subset/FINDINGS.md) also identify an inverted control: calibrated hedging's positive pole is appropriate uncertainty while its negative pole is blanket overconfidence. Its transfer sign should never be silently relabeled as moral sycophancy.

The older `results/main` suite repeats the system-prompt method with Llama-3-8B-Instruct on 200 Perez questions (160 train / 40 holdout). It is a **different model and smaller experiment**; activations and AUCs should be reported separately. The later matched-backend [dataset-control pilot](DATASET_CONTROL_V1.md) used 200 unused Perez questions and 200 Databricks Dolly questions, each split 120 train / 80 report, with the original 20 prompt pairs fixed. Each dataset was both a training source and a transfer target. The [Dolly prompt-wording follow-up](PROMPT_WORDING_CONTROL_V1.txt) has a frozen manifest and paraphrases but no generated paraphrase arm or new fitted probes yet.

### Two general probing lines: system-prompted and behavior-labeled

There are **two different ways this repository attempted a general detector**. The second is the non-system-prompted line: its labels come from judged behavior on ordinary task prompts, not from assigning a positive or negative experimental system instruction. Both need explicit rows in comparisons with moral-specific and taxonomy probes; their scores cannot be pooled because they use different labels and model checkpoints.

| General probe | In-distribution training and holdout | What is already known about transfer |
|---|---|---|
| `general_baseline`, saved as `universal` in the **Llama-3.1 system-prompt suite** | Its positive instruction asks the model to prioritize user approval over accuracy; its negative instruction reverses that priority. It uses the same 1,600/400 Perez question split as the other 19 cells. At response L16, the saved logistic holdout AUC is **1.000** (0.99998 unrounded); the matched DIM holdout AUC is **0.930**. | After selection on separate target examples, the frozen logistic probe reports AUC **0.716** on SyPR; **0.657**, **0.631**, and **0.597** on AITA `nta_when_yta`, `unwarranted_nta`, and `both_nta`; and **0.664**, **0.594**, and **0.634** on ELEPHANT validation, indirectness, and framing. These are behavioral OOD tests, not additional ID scores. See the [SyPR](results/llama31_5k_subset/analysis/matrix_sypr.json), [AITA](results/llama31_5k_subset/analysis/matrix_moral.json), and [ELEPHANT](results/llama31_5k_subset/analysis/matrix_elephant.json) matrices. |
| **Four-category behavioral mixture, trained linear probe**, fitted on **Llama-3 activations** | Pools judged SyPR praise, Are You Sure caving, social sycophancy, and AITA moral verdict labels, balanced to 355 examples per label per category. The first line is a **single broad instruction contrast** within the 20-cell experiment. The other two use a **pooled behavioral source** without that induced contrast. For the pooled source, SyPR, Are You Sure, social, and moral are **all ID categories**; only a genuinely unseen category, dataset, or prompt regime is category/source OOD. Neither a held-out row from those four categories nor steering on those same categories establishes OOD transfer.

## 2. Dataset roles for the 20-cell suite

“In distribution” is relative to a particular trained probe. The main suite's in-distribution source is Perez questions answered under its **own** contrastive system-prompt pair. A different cell on new Perez questions is a *new instruction condition*, not a new natural dataset. Dolly becomes in distribution for a probe trained on Dolly in the dataset-control pilot.

| Dataset or family | Role in prior 20-cell work | Target / status |
|---|---|---|
| Perez model-written-evals questions | **ID** for the main Llama-3.1 suite; 1,600 train and 400 within-pool holdout questions, crossed with each of 20 pairs. | Instruction-polarity label; full 5,000-question generation pool exists. |
| Held-out Perez questions under *other* system-prompt cells | **Instruction OOD**, while user-question source and synthetic labeling remain the same. | Cross-cell AUC on a sample drawn from the 3,000-question reserve; 20 × 19 directed transfers exclude each probe's own cell. See [cross-cell evaluator](probing/analyze_probes/eval_cross_cell.py). |
| Databricks Dolly 15k sample | **Question-source OOD** for Perez-trained probes; **ID** for Dolly-trained probes in the matched dataset control. | 200 questions across eight categories; 120/80 split. Original system-prompt wording only in the completed pilot. See [results](results/llama31_dataset_control_v1/analysis/RESULTS.md). |
| ELEPHANT social examples: OEQ, SS, and AITA-YTA | **Behavioral OOD** for the 20-cell prompt probes. | Judge-derived validation, indirectness, and framing labels; 8,797 extracted responses in the Llama-3.1 run. These are social dimensions, not the AITA NTA/NTA moral outcome. See [evaluator](probing/analyze_probes/eval_social_sycophancy.py). |

The external target datasets above contain model responses and judgments produced under their own setups; they are not responses generated by the 20 training system prompts. For the ELEPHANT, AITA, and SyPR scoring runs, configurations were selected on 30% of grouped target examples and reported on the remaining 70%. Pair sides and repeated turns remain grouped where applicable. The recorded Llama-3.1 best report AUCs are 0.653 on AITA and 0.806 on SyPR; the high ID AUCs should not be substituted for either. [SYCON results](results/llama31_5k_subset/analysis/syconbench/RESULTS.md) vary by target, from 0.463 to 0.740 for the selected probes, with question-bootstrap intervals.

### Separate behavior-labeled probe track

The repository also has an older **Llama-3-8B-Instruct activation/probe** track under [`tool_calling/tasks/sycophancy/scripts`](../tool_calling/tasks/sycophancy/scripts). Its training labels come from judged model behavior, not the 20 system-prompt polarities. These are ordinary trained linear probes: the [probe implementation](https://github.com/oscaryas/SycoScope/blob/b56b6b4/tool_calling/tasks/sycophancy/sycophancy_probes.py) fits a PyTorch `LinearProbe` with weighted binary cross-entropy and reports stratified cross-validation; the separately saved mixture holdout refits on training rows only. There are **individual baseline probes** for SyPR, Are You Sure, social, and moral behavior, plus a pooled general probe. Its four-category [balanced mixture](../tool_calling/tasks/sycophancy/scripts/build_sycophancy_mixture.py) contains SyPR unwarranted praise, Are You Sure caving, social sycophancy from OEQ/SS/AITA responses, and moral AITA-NTA-FLIP NTA/NTA outcomes. The saved mixture has 2,840 rows: 355 positive and 355 negative per category. For the **pooled mixture probe**, all four categories are ID; the [stratified holdout](../tool_calling/tasks/sycophancy/results/probing/mixture_holdout_eval/summary.json) has 2,272 train and 568 test rows, with layer-14 accuracy 0.805 overall and 0.725 on its moral slice. This was a row-level category/label split, so it should not be treated as a guaranteed conflict-group holdout without further audit. The SyPR and Are You Sure source summaries identify **Llama-3.1** generation while this mixture's saved probe run used **Llama-3** activation extraction. That is an off-policy source-model mismatch to audit before interpreting the mixture as one model's behavior.

For **single-category behavioral probes**, the category used to fit a probe is ID and other categories are OOD: an OEQ social probe was checked on other social and AITA sources; a SyPR probe was checked on AITA-NTA-FLIP, AITA-NTA-OG, AITA-YTA, OEQ, and SS. Some of these experiments used *steered changes in judged behavior* as their transfer outcome rather than frozen-probe AUC, so their numbers are not interchangeable with the 20-cell scoring table. The moral subset originally concatenated original and flipped stories into one length-capped input; [the corrected extraction](../tool_calling/tasks/sycophancy/scripts/mixture_residual_probe_pipeline_v2.py) processes the two sides separately and averages their states. The [branch-only general DIM implementation](https://github.com/oscaryas/SycoScope/blob/b56b6b4/tool_calling/tasks/sycophancy/scripts/mixture_dim_pipeline.py) uses that corrected moral pooling. This track is useful for the proposed matched trained-linear-probe-versus-DIM comparison, provided the same conflict grouping and data-fidelity rules are used for both methods.

## 3. Prior difference-in-means (DIM) attempts

DIM uses the unit vector from the mean activation for label 0 to the mean activation for label 1; a midpoint between training projection means supplies a classifier threshold. Direction sign and the meaning of label 1 depend on the source dataset. These attempts are distinct and cannot be pooled across the two Llama checkpoints.

## 4. Proposed next experiment

1. **Freeze an AITA fidelity audit and labels.** Select conflicts by ID before analysis. Review original/flipped factual equivalence blind to model verdict, annotate the decisive events, and retain a prespecified high-fidelity subset. Record audit disagreements and exclusions. Use the article's four rewrite-error categories as the starting coding guide.
2. **Separate the mechanisms.** On each conflict, distinguish the actual narrator from the user expressing an opinion. Collect neutral, NTA-opinion, and YTA-opinion conditions on the same facts and model. Record verdict, which actor the verdict refers to, and whether the response endorses the narrator or follows the user's expressed opinion. Treat unclear outcomes as such.
3. **Fit matched probe and DIM baselines, including the non-system-prompted general line.** Keep all perspectives, nudges, paraphrases, and samples of one conflict in one split. Fit linear-classifier and DIM directions on the same training conflicts and at matched layers/readouts, including a last-prompt readout for advance prediction and response readouts for detection. Compare (a) the frozen system-prompt `general_baseline`, (b) the general behavior-labeled mixture linear probe, (c) the general behavior-labeled mixture DIM, and (d) moral-specific counterparts, all on the same report conflicts and with model checkpoints matched or explicitly separated. Refit the branch-only general DIM with a sufficiently sized, grouped holdout; select hyperparameters and layers inside training/validation only.
4. **Test on genuinely new material.** Report high-fidelity held-out AITA first; then test on separately sourced moral conflicts and the existing ELEPHANT, SyPR, and SYCON behavioral targets. Report each target separately with grouped bootstrap intervals, polarity-aware AUC, and simple verdict/text baselines. Dolly and cross-cell transfer can quantify prompt and question-source robustness but cannot replace behavioral validation.
5. **Check direction meaning before steering.** Compare the trained probe and DIM directions, inspect examples with large disagreements, and test warranted agreement, justified praise, and correct narrator condemnation as negative controls. A steering follow-up should use a held-out conflict set, predeclared direction and strength grid, and independent behavioral judgments; avoid choosing a strength from the report set.

The immediate decision point is whether an AITA direction predicts **user-opinion-dependent endorsement on fact-preserving conflicts**, above a verdict-only baseline and without firing on justified agreement. If it does, DIM versus a matched trained linear probe becomes an informative method comparison. If it only separates NTA/NTA from mixed verdicts, the narrower finding is verdict-pattern decodability.
